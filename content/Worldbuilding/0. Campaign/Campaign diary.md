# Session 1 - An Inciting Event - 22 Sep 2024

## Introduction

The campaign has four players, and we are using the free archetype rules, allowing most of the characters to be multi-classed.

### Characters

- **Rufus** (Minotaur Fighter-Champion): A loyal soldier of the [[Adamant Ratio]], 5th iteration (a middle rank).
- **Niko** (Human Weretiger Swashbuckler-Sorcerer): A refugee who arrived a few weeks ago and now works as the camp chef’s assistant.
- **Ishmael** (Minotaur Witch): A member of the esteemed [[Travelling College]], who regularly visits the camp. Their college caravan recently left.
- **Beitris** (Human Geniekin Psionicist): A young nomad and ward of Ishmael, who joined the college’s caravan a few weeks ago and remains to journey with Ishmael.

## GM Thoughts: Sandbox and Structure

I am striving to run this campaign as an emergent sandbox with an old-school feel, allowing the players to shape the direction of the story based on their choices. While I want to foster this open-ended play, for now, I’m asking the group to stay on my planned rails until we reach [[Durinson's Rest]]. This first phase acts as a tutorial for all of us, giving the players time to familiarize themselves with the rules. It also gives me time to learn the system, understand more about the players, and develop the sandbox.

## Setting the Scene

The story began at [[C.L. Patient Ordinal]], an Adamant Ratio mobile camp about to be sanctified as a permanent location. Tomorrow, a sacred "origin stone" would be installed, symbolising the camp’s transition to permanence.

One of the players said in Session 0 that she likes interesting descriptions, so I went hard on the opener:

> The sun rises over Ondra, casting light on Caster Lemma Patient Ordinal, a mobile camp of the Adamant Ratio stationed in the north.
> This camp is a mobile settlement, the Ratio’s equivalent of a ship, and its pronouns are xe/xyr. Like all Ratio locations, it emulates the spiral. At the center is an inner stone chamber, sealed with a heavy wooden door. One iteration out, a semi-square stockade wall forms the camp’s first defense. The spiral continues outward with stone markers and lighter defensive installations. Among them are tent encampments and Ratio soldiers on patrol.
> The camp isn’t only for the Ratio's devoted; refugees and visitors are also welcome, provided they submit to a magical screening and work for their stay after three days.
> Here, we have:
> < I said the character descriptions form above >
> Despite these colorful figures, the camp is typically dour, serious, and focused.
> Today, however, there is a nervous, almost celebratory atmosphere. Tomorrow, C.L. Patient Ordinal will end its mobile life and be sanctified as a permanent settlement. This is a rare event, showing the strategic importance of this position along the trade routes—especially due to the ores, relics, and knowledge flowing from Durinson’s Rest to the south.
> A new **Origin Stone** will be placed at the camp’s center, inside the inner chamber. Though called a stone, it’s actually a precious, magically imbued cone made from a mixture of mithril and adamantine. This stone will begin the spiral that forms the camp’s boundaries and life, offering a durable source of magical power for operations... and defenses.
> Everyone in the camp is preparing for the ceremonies tomorrow.

I then used a prompt tool to establish character pairs (Neko and Rufus, Beitris and Ishmael), asking them share three things about themselves the other PC would already know about them. This sparked great in-character role-playing, which fleshed out their personalities and relationships.

### Character Moments

- Rufus showed a rule-following, disciplined nature but was frustrated by others being looser with the rules.
- Niko secretly snuck out beers at lunch for the group.
- Ishmael distributed almanacs and engaged others for their stories.
- Beitris eagerly talked to everyone and expressed excitement about the origin stone due to her affinity for metal.

### Backstories Revealed

- **Beitris** openly discussed visions of strange whales under a starry sky, though she doesn’t fully understand what they mean. These [[Skywhales]] seem to guide her to join the Travelling College.
- **Ishmael** has been tasked by the Travelling College to investigate [[Durinson's Rest]], a nearby settlement on the road to [[The Maw]], sensing big things ahead in the region.
- **Niko** hasn’t shared her full backstory yet, but the camp knows she is a refugee from a shipwreck.
- **Rufus** has a mid-rank position in the Adamant Ratio, known for his talent in enclosed spaces. His old friend, [[Bettina Ironsworn]], a 4th iteration Dux Custos (captain of the guard), helped secure his current post.

## Exploring the Camp

The group began to explore the camp.

- **Beitris** sought out an audience with **Jucundus**, the Origo Custos (keeper of the origin), the warmhearted leader overseeing the sanctification.
- **Rufus** noticed a broken ballista and, after some investigation, connected with **Valeria Audux**, the Armatura (in charge of defenses). He learned that ballistae had been mysteriously malfunctioning. This is due to infiltration by the [[Escape]] faction, though the group didn’t piece this together yet.
- **Niko** continued her sneaky ways, smuggling out lunch ales, which **Rufus** reported to Bettina. Bettina didn’t take it too seriously.
- **Ishmael** talked to others, giving out almanacs and gathering stories.
- Beitris charmed camp members, and the camp smith dropped a clue about a minister from the [[Concord of Sovereign Houses of Ondra]] (COSHO) visiting a month ago. The smith hinted that COSHO could have influence over the region.

## Nightsong and the Attack

At Nightsong (midnight), when the dark planet [[Var]] reaches its apogee, the camp rose for peaceful storytelling.

- Soldiers performed a silent play recounting a hero of the Ratio, though **Ishmael** recognized the story as much older.
- A stablehand, **Fraster**, led some drumming, but this marked the beginning of an attack.

The camp was ambushed by the **Escape**, a mysterious faction not yet known to the camp.

- Fraster revealed himself to be one of them, summoning a **razorworm**, a giant burrowing creature, with his drum. A second razorworm attacked the chamber where the origin was kept.
- **Bettina** tasked the PCs with handling the worm summoned by Fraster, creating a focused encounter. I introduced a “clock” mechanic from PbTA games, advancing whenever certain conditions were met.
- The worm quickly broke through a wall, allowing cultists to enter. The PCs fought carefully: **Rufus** tanked, **Ishmael** used a repaired ballista, and the group realized Fraster controlled the worm with his drum. Taking Fraster down destabilized the creature, leading to its eventual retreat.

## Closing the Session

The fight ended when the worm fled, dropping **Rufus** from its jaws after a well-placed ballista bolt from **Ishmael**. The group captured one cultist, and the clock advanced to 3 out of 4, meaning the Escape raiders had made off with some of their prize, and Jucundus was gravely injured, but the origin stone was safe.

Next time, the group will investigate the captured cultist and potentially find a tattoo marking them as an Escape member. Bettina will send them to **Durinson’s Rest** to pursue the faction further, giving the PCs their next mission.

## Reflections

I am really happy with how much the players role-played and I feel like I created an atmosphere for immersion. I think they were struggling a little with the rule set, even the person who is a huge fan of Pathfinder 2 and owns all the books. But ultimately I think it wil be on ok system for this group.

I still have fears that the more open-ended sandbox style play will feel aimless, but I feel less fearful about that having seen this group really lean into immersive roleplay. Their characters feel well-developed already.

My next focus will be on creating the sandbox. I want to:

- Refine my random tables so that the hexploration provides a balance of interesting encounters with different factions or other forces in the world, and fragments of lore that grab their interest
- Even though we are on rails, I want there to be a few ways they can get to Durinson's rest and not just follow the road. I will try to offer some hooks. I am planning on putting a necromancer person in the mountains east of the road but south of Durinson's Rest, so they could do that side quest along the way.
- I feel like I need to develop other factions more and understand their goals, and then have them more clearly expressed in the world.
- I am trying to remember to be open to my players surprising me and saying yes to their plans even if I don't know how I will support those plans.
- I am also trying to remember to be really overt with hooks and clues and lore, because I am tempted to drip-feed the mystery, but it's so easy to underestimate how hard it is for them to piece together the picture that is so clear in my mind
